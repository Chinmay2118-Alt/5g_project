# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper introduces RAP-FedWMMSE, a distributed, model-driven beamforming and resource allocation framework for two-phase half-duplex decode-and-forward (DF) relay-assisted cell-free massive MIMO networks. The proposed architecture decouples slow-timescale capacitated binary relay–user matching and phase-split ($\tau$) adaptation from fast-timescale deep-unfolded per-node WMMSE layers, where role-specific neural models predict layer-wise step sizes, relaxation, regularization, and initial power shares alongside primal–dual QoS/DF bottleneck pricing and personalized federated parameter sharing under fronthaul constraints. Through an extensive evaluation across 14 experiments, paired ablations, and certified global spatial branch-and-bound benchmarks, the authors rigorously decompose the sources of performance gains and transparently report both positive findings and negative results.

## Strengths
* The paper exhibits exceptional experimental rigor and scientific transparency by providing paired statistical comparisons across 100 drops, certifying global optimality gaps via spatial branch-and-bound on a small network, and candidly reporting negative results where federated learning, cooperative multi-relay forwarding, and single-antenna learned models did not support the initial hypotheses.
* The two-timescale architectural decomposition—separating slow-timescale QoS-priced capacitated relay matching and concave 1D phase-split ($\tau$) optimization from fast-timescale distributed WMMSE updates smoothed via a log-sum-exp soft-min—effectively renders a coupled nonconvex MINLP tractable at an order-of-magnitude lower computational runtime than centralized conic SCA solvers.
* The paired ablation methodology cleanly isolates the exact contributions of inter-node fast coordination ($+7.30$ PF utility), training-free slow-timescale adaptation ($+3.12$ to $+3.40$), and learned unfolded layer parameters ($+0.61$ to $+0.89$), offering valuable diagnostic clarity into how distributed deep unfolding behaves in multi-hop cell-free regimes.

## Weaknesses
* The empirical ablations indicate that the core neural unfolding and federated learning components provide minimal practical advantage over classical mechanics: the learned parameters add only $+0.61$ to $+0.89$ PF utility (mainly accelerating 6 layers to match 10 untrained damped layers while falling short of 100 untrained damped layers at $5.54$ utility) and cause convergence instability and user starvation (14% of drops) on the single-antenna MINLP benchmark, whereas prior distributed WMMSE unfolding works also achieved topology generalization via graph neural networks rather than topology-locked MLPs (Reference Paper: Coordinated Sum-Rate Maximization in Multicell MU-MIMO With Deep Unrolling, Lukas Schynol and Marius Pesavento, 2023; Deep Graph Unfolding for QoS-Aware Distributed Beamforming in Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Tianwei Yang et al., 2026).
* The personalized federated learning module yields zero measurable network utility gain ($-0.06 \pm 0.19$ nats) over non-federated training (`no-FL`) because each node trains on unlimited synthetic channel realizations from a local deployment simulator and receives globally coupled gradients via centralized backpropagation across all nodes' layers without counting backward coordination signaling against the fronthaul budget (Reference Paper: Federated Learning for Precoding Design in Cell-Free Massive MIMO Systems, Dong Wang et al., 2023; Towards Personalized Federated Learning, Alysa Ziying Tan et al., 2023).
* The evaluation relies on several asymmetric problem formulations and baseline choices: RAP-FedWMMSE optimizes a QoS-penalized relaxation with Monte Carlo draws from the true CSI-error distribution while centralized WMMSE-SCA and exact MINLP baselines optimize unconstrained certainty-equivalent PF utility, and the $+7.30$ coordination gain is anchored against an unregularized `Local-WMMSE` baseline that omits statistical coordination such as Team MMSE and performs worse than one-shot RZF (Reference Paper: Team MMSE Precoding With Applications to Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Lorenzo Miretti, Emil Björnson, and David Gesbert, 2022; Multipair DF Relaying With Network-Assisted Full-Duplex Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Tung Thanh Vu et al., 2023).
* Several physical-layer modeling choices distort the resource trade-offs, including penalizing unweighted sum transmit power $P_{\text{tot}}$ rather than duty-cycle-weighted block energy $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$ in the optimization objective (which over-penalizes Phase II relay power by $\frac{\tau}{1-\tau} = 4\times$ at $\tau=0.8$ and leaves $\tau$ adaptation blind to duty-cycle energy), omitting per-block fast coordination traffic ($\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$, which exceeds full raw CSI upload at $N_A=N_R=2$) from the fronthaul budget, and enforcing full-message decoding at every assigned relay without direct-link fallback.

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Framing, System Model, and Problem Formulation] (Pages: 1-3)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Physical Mismatch Between the Unweighted Objective Power Model ($P_{\text{tot}}$) and the Two-Phase Half-Duplex Duty Cycle ($\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$) (Section III-C, Eqs. 7–8; Section IV-A, Eq. 9a; Section VIII-I)**
   - **Verification Summary:** The channel matrix and vector dimensions ($\mathbf{h}_{mk} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_A}$, $\mathbf{G}_{mr} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_R \times N_A}$, $\mathbf{g}_{rk} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_R}$), Hermitian transpose structures, noise normalizations ($\sigma^2 = \sigma_r^2 = 1$), SINR formulations in Equations (1)–(3), and two-phase achievable spectral efficiency formulations in Equations (4)–(6) were checked from first principles and are mathematically consistent with standard half-duplex decode-and-forward (DF) relaying under independent Gaussian codebook re-encoding.
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section III-C, the network power expression penalized inside the optimization objective of problem $\text{P0}$ (Equation 9a) is defined in Equation (7) as the unweighted sum of per-phase power draws:
     $$P_{\text{tot}} = \sum_m \left( \frac{1}{\eta_A} \sum_k \|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2 + P_A^c \right) + \sum_r b_r \left( \frac{1}{\eta_R} \sum_k \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2 + P_R^c \right)$$
     Because access points (APs) transmit only during Phase I (of duration $\tau$) and half-duplex relays transmit only during Phase II (of duration $1 - \tau$), summing the unweighted per-phase transmit powers $\frac{1}{\eta_A}\sum_k \|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2$ and $\frac{1}{\eta_R}\sum_k \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2$ treats both sets of nodes as if they transmitted simultaneously for the entire unit-length coherence block, rather than reflecting the true time-averaged power or block energy $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$ defined in Equation (8). While Section III-C and Limitation (vi) explicitly note this distinction and report $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$ alongside $P_{\text{tot}}$ in the energy evaluation (Section VIII-I, where penalizing $P_{\text{tot}}$ reverses the power ordering between relay-assisted methods and the no-relay benchmark at $\lambda_E = 0$: $17.4\text{ W}$ and $17.7\text{ W}$ vs. $20.9\text{ W}$), embedding $P_{\text{tot}}$ rather than $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$ directly into $\text{P0}$ (Equation 9a) and the unfolded updates (12)–(15) introduces two structural distortions whenever the energy price $\lambda_E > 0$:
     1. *Asymmetric penalization of relay vs. AP transmit power inside the fast-timescale unfolded updates:* In $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$, AP transmit power is weighted by $\tau$ and relay transmit power is weighted by $1-\tau$, and inside the local gradient (12), curvature (13), and augmented utility (15), the rate terms are weighted by the phase durations ($\tau$ in Phase I and $1-\tau$ in Phase II via the link weights $A_k^{(1)}, A_{rk}^{AR}, A_k^{(2)}$). Because $P_{\text{tot}}$ omits these duty-cycle weights, optimizing $P_{\text{tot}}$ applies identical per-Watt penalties ($\frac{\lambda_E}{\eta_A}\mathbf{w}_{mj}$ and $\frac{\lambda_E}{\eta_R}\mathbf{v}_{rj}$, independent of $\tau$) to Phase I and Phase II beams inside the unfolded WMMSE gradient and curvature updates. At the median adapted phase split $\tau = 0.8$ ($1-\tau = 0.2$), a Watt of relay transmit power lasting for $20\%$ of the block consumes four times less actual block energy than a Watt of AP transmit power lasting for $80\%$ of the block (given $\eta_A = \eta_R = 0.4$), yet is penalized at the exact same rate inside $\text{P0}$ and (12)–(13)—effectively over-penalizing Phase II relay transmit power by a factor of $\frac{\tau}{1-\tau} = 4$ relative to Phase I AP transmit power (and by $\frac{1}{1-\tau} = 5\times$ relative to true block energy $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$), disproportionately throttling relay forwarding as $\lambda_E$ increases.
     2. *Decoupling of phase-split adaptation from power consumption:* In $\text{P0}$, $\tau$ is a joint decision variable alongside the beams, and in Step 2 (Section V-B) and the centralized benchmark, $\tau$ is updated by maximizing Equation (9a). Because $P_{\text{tot}}$ is invariant to $\tau$, the phase-split search is completely insensitive to the power asymmetry between operating $M=8$ APs in Phase I versus at most $R=2$ relays in Phase II, even though shifting $\tau$ directly alters the true block energy $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Replacing $P_{\text{tot}}$ with the duty-cycle-weighted block energy $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$ directly in Equation (9a) (which only requires scaling the AP and relay transmit-power penalty multipliers by $\tau$ and $1-\tau$, respectively, and evaluating $\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}$ during the $\tau$ grid search) would make the optimization formulation physically consistent with the half-duplex frame structure. Alternatively, the text in Sections III-C, IV-A, and VIII-I could explicitly note that using $P_{\text{tot}}$ in Equation (9a) and (12)–(13) weights Phase II relay power by $\frac{\tau}{1-\tau}$ relative to actual energy consumption when $\tau \neq 0.5$.

2. **Decode-and-Forward Rate Bottleneck Without Direct-Link Fallback and Multi-Relay Coherence Idealizations (Section III-B, Eqs. 3–6, Assumptions ii–iii)**
   - **Potential Concern:** The achievable DF rate in Equation (6), $r_k = \min\big\{C_k^D, \min_{r: a_{rk}=1} C_{rk}^{AR}\big\}$, together with modeling assumptions (ii) and (iii) in Section III-B, introduces two physical-layer and information-theoretic modeling tensions:
     1. *Absence of direct-link decoding fallback (selection DF) or per-block relay muting:* During Phase I, UE $k$ directly observes the AP transmission with spectral efficiency $C_k^{(1)} = \tau \log_2(1 + \gamma_k^{(1)}) \le C_k^D$ independently of whether an assigned relay successfully decodes the message. Under Equation (6) and assumption (iii), the bottleneck $\min_{r: a_{rk}=1} C_{rk}^{AR}$ is conditioned strictly on the static epoch-level binary association indicator $a_{rk} = 1$ (fixed from large-scale gains and relaxed by QoS dual prices in Step 1) rather than on active Phase II forwarding ($\|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\| > 0$). Consequently, if instantaneous small-scale fading within an epoch degrades an assigned relay hop below the direct link ($C_{rk}^{AR} < C_k^{(1)}$), or if a second, weaker relay is assigned under $L_{\max} > 1$, Equation (6) forces UE $k$'s rate below what it already achieves by decoding the Phase I direct signal alone—and even setting $\mathbf{v}_{rk} = \mathbf{0}$ inside the beamformer cannot remove a bottlenecked relay from the minimum in Equation (6).
     2. *Coherent Phase II multi-relay combining over low-rate control links:* For cooperation orders $L_{\max} > 1$, Equation (3) models all assisting relays ($a_{rk}=1$) as forming a phase-synchronized distributed antenna array whose signals add coherently in amplitude at UE $k$, $\left|\sum_r \mathbf{g}_{rk}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{v}_{rk}\right|^2$. While assumption (ii) candidly notes that carrier phase synchronization across relays with only low-rate control links ($F_r^{\text{ctrl}} = 10\text{ Mbit/s}$, Section IV-B) is an idealization, coherent combining at the UE also requires complex baseband phase alignment $\arg(\mathbf{g}_{rk}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{v}_{rk})$ across the distributed relays on every coherence block. For RAP-FedWMMSE, this baseband phase alignment is implicitly supplied by the per-layer fast coordination vector $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ (which broadcasts the aggregate Phase II gain $S_{kj}^{(2)} = \sum_r \mathbf{g}_{rk}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{v}_{rj}$), whereas non-iterative baselines without inter-relay phase coordination (such as MRT at the relays) would physically achieve only non-coherent power addition $\sum_r |\mathbf{g}_{rk}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{v}_{rk}|^2$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in Section III-B why the receiver/beamformer cannot fall back to direct Phase I decoding $C_k^{(1)} = \tau \log_2(1+\gamma_k^{(1)})$ by muting a bottlenecked relay ($\mathbf{v}_{rk} = \mathbf{0}$) within a block (i.e., writing $r_k = \max\big\{\tau \log_2(1+\gamma_k^{(1)}),\, \min\{C_k^D, \min_{r: a_{rk}=1, \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|>0} C_{rk}^{AR}\}\big\}$ under selection DF), and note that baseband phase alignment for $L_{\max} > 1$ in Equation (3) depends on per-block phase coordination feedback such as the $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ exchange.

3. **Objective, Constraint, and Timescale Asymmetry in Problem Formulation $\text{P0}$ Between RAP-FedWMMSE and the Optimization Baselines (Section IV-A, Eqs. 9a–9g; Section IV-B)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section IV-A formulates the canonical per-block design problem $\text{P0}$ as a joint MINLP over $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{A}, \tau)$ combining a certainty-equivalent objective on $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ (Equation 9a) with a conditional QoS outage probability constraint over the true channel $\mathbf{H} \mid \hat{\mathbf{H}}$, $\Pr_{\mathbf{H}|\hat{\mathbf{H}}}\{r_k < R^{\min}\} \le \epsilon_k$ (Equation 9f, which reduces to $r_k \ge R^{\min}$ under perfect CSI). As transparently disclosed in the second paragraph of Section IV-A and in Section IV-B, none of the evaluated algorithms solve $\text{P0}$ as written:
     1. All optimization baselines (Centralized WMMSE-SCA/AO, Centralized WMMSE no-relay, Local-WMMSE, and the Exact MINLP spatial branch-and-bound solver) drop constraint (9f) entirely and maximize the unconstrained proportional-fair (PF) utility (Equation 9a).
     2. RAP-FedWMMSE replaces constraint (9f) with a primal–dual rate-slack penalty ($\lambda_k^Q$), DF bottleneck prices ($\nu_{rk}$), and a smoothed stochastic CSI-error outage surrogate evaluated at $\rho = 0.98$ (which remains active during training even in perfect-CSI experiments), and uses $\lambda_k^Q$ to modify the slow-timescale relay association $\mathbf{A}$.
     3. With respect to timescales, Centralized WMMSE-SCA/AO optimizes $\tau$ on every coherence block (fast timescale) with zero-price association $\mathbf{A}$, whereas RAP-FedWMMSE fixes both $\mathbf{A}$ and $\tau$ across an entire epoch of duration $T_e$ (slow timescale).
     4. Inside Equation (9f), the notation $\Pr_{\mathbf{H}|\hat{\mathbf{H}}}\{r_k < R^{\min}\} \le \epsilon_k$ leaves implicit that the beams and receive combiners $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U})$ inside $r_k$ are held fixed to the estimates computed from $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ rather than re-optimized for $\mathbf{H}$ (this is only specified later in Section V-E).
   - **Impact and Suggested Improvement:** While evaluating all methods on the baselines' unconstrained PF utility is candidly explained, this formulation asymmetry complicates the direct interpretation of the optimality gaps to the certified MINLP bound and centralized benchmark introduced in the Abstract and Sections I–II: RAP-FedWMMSE is trained to sacrifice pure PF utility on strong users to satisfy soft QoS constraints and guard against synthetic $\rho = 0.98$ channel errors that are absent in perfect-CSI tests (as reflected in Section VIII-G, where removing the QoS machinery reduces the small-network optimality gap from $1.119$ to $1.059$). Explicitly separating the unconstrained baseline problem $\text{P0}_{\text{PF}}$ (Equations 9a–9e, 9g) from the QoS-penalized relaxation in Section IV-A, writing the rate dependence in Equation (9f) explicitly as $r_k(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{A}, \tau; \mathbf{H}) < R^{\min}$ with $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U})$ fixed from $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$, and either including a pure-PF ablation of RAP-FedWMMSE ($\lambda_k^Q = 0, \lambda_O = 0$) in the main comparison or incorporating the same soft QoS penalty into the centralized SCA benchmark would isolate the intrinsic optimization performance of the unfolded architecture from the utility trade-off induced by the QoS and robustness penalties.

4. **Exclusion of Fast Per-Layer Coordination Signaling ($\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$) from the Fronthaul and Relay Control-Link Constraints (Section I; Section III-B; Section IV-B, Eq. 10)**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section IV-B, the per-epoch traffic constraint on AP fronthaul links ($F_m^{\max}$) and relay control links ($F_r^{\text{ctrl}}$) is formulated in Equation (10) as:
     $$D_m^{\text{FL}} \le \big[F_m^{\max} T_e - D_m^{\text{data}}\big]_+, \qquad D_r^{\text{FL}} \le F_r^{\text{ctrl}} T_e$$
     where $D_m^{\text{data}} = B T_e \sum_k c_{mk} \bar{r}_k$. While Section IV-B explicitly states the modeling choice that $D_m^{\text{data}} > F_m^{\max} T_e$ gates only federated learning (FL) participation without throttling user data rates, Equation (10) also assumes that the relay control link $F_r^{\text{ctrl}} T_e$ (and the residual AP fronthaul) is untouched by the fast coordination vector $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ exchanged at every unfolded layer $\ell = 1, \dots, L$ of every coherence block, as well as the slow vector $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{s}}(t)$ exchanged each epoch.
   - **Impact and Suggested Improvement:** As noted in Section I ("At our small antenna counts this exchange costs more than uploading all CSI") and quantified in Section V-D/H ($352L = 2,112$ complex scalars per block at $L=6$, or $\approx 67.6\text{–}135.2\text{ kbit/block}$ at 16–32 bits per real entry), every relay and AP must exchange $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ six times per coherence block. Across standard millisecond-scale coherence blocks ($10^2\text{–}10^3\text{ blocks/s}$ within a $T_e = 1\text{ s}$ epoch), transporting $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ over the wired control/fronthaul links would require tens of Mbit/s of continuous throughput—saturating or exceeding the entire $F_r^{\text{ctrl}} = 10\text{ Mbit/s}$ relay control link capacity before any FL traffic $D_r^{\text{FL}}$ could occur. Explicitly stating in Section IV-B alongside Equation (10) that per-block $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ signaling is omitted from the fronthaul/control-link budget (or assumed to be acquired via over-the-air precoded pilots rather than the capacity-limited links in Equation 10, as mentioned later in Section V-D) would make the system-level traffic accounting self-contained and consistent across Sections I, III, and IV.

1. **Distinction Between Single-Layer Envelope-Theorem Partial Gradients and Multi-Layer End-to-End Backpropagation Through $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$, $c_k$, and $\rho_k$ (Section V-D, Eqs. 12–13):**
   - **Verification Summary:** All symbolic and numerical dimensionality and byte counts across Sections V-C, V-D, V-G, and V-H were verified to be algebraically exact: the slow vector $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{s}}(t)$ has $4K + RK = 48$ entries; the AP and relay inputs have $K N_A + R N_R N_A + 6K + RK = 88$ and $M N_R N_A + K N_R + 6K + RK = 112$ entries; the backbone $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ has $(32 \times 64 + 64) + (64 \times 64 + 64) + (64 \times 32 + 32) = 8,352$ float32 parameters ($33,408\text{ B} = S^\downarrow$); the fast coordination vector $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ contains $2K^2 + 4K + R(K^2 + 2K + K N_R) = 352$ scalars per layer ($2,112$ at $L = 6$); the sparse upload payload is $S^\uparrow = 836 \times 8\text{ B} + 4(K+4)\text{ B} = 6,736\text{ B}$ at $K=8$; and raw CSI collection requires $M K N_A + M R N_R N_A + R K N_R = 224$ complex values.
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section V-D (page 4), there is a subtle mathematical tension between two statements describing how the intermediate WMMSE auxiliary variables—specifically the normalized MMSE relay combiner $\mathbf{u}_{rk} = \tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{rk} / \|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{rk}\|$ with $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{rk} = (\sum_j \mathbf{f}_{rj}\mathbf{f}_{rj}^{\mathrm{H}} + (\sigma_r^2 + \epsilon')\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{f}_{rk}$, the equalizers $c_k$, and the MSE weights $\rho_k = 1/(\varepsilon_k + \epsilon')$—are differentiated:
     1. Under *Fast coordination vector*: *"At the start of every layer these quantities are recomputed from the current beams... During training, gradients flow through all of these, including $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$."*
     2. Under *Local gradient and curvature*: Equation (12) gives the closed-form expression for $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}_{mj}^*}\mathcal{L}$ treating the effective relay channel $\mathbf{b}_{mrk} = \mathbf{G}_{mr}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{u}_{rk}$ as fixed with respect to $\mathbf{w}_{mj}^*$, followed by the claim: *"A unit test confirms that (12) equals the autograd gradient of the global loss to machine precision."*
   - **Mathematical Analysis and Actionable Improvement:** Equation (12) is the partial Wirtinger derivative of the WMMSE surrogate loss with respect to $\mathbf{w}_{mj}^*$ holding $c_k^{(1)}, c_{rk}^{AR}$, $\rho_k^{(1)}, \rho_{rk}^{AR}$, $\pi_k^D, \pi_{rk}^{AR}$, and $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$ fixed. By the envelope theorem, omitting the chain-rule Jacobian $\partial \mathbf{u}_{rk} / \partial \mathbf{w}_{mj}^*$ is exact at a single layer as $\epsilon' \to 0$ because the combiner $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$ is stream-specific—appearing only in stream $k$'s own relay-hop SINR $\gamma_{rk}^{AR}$ in Equation (2) and not in $\gamma_{rj}^{AR}$ for $j \neq k$—and $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{rk}$ maximizes the generalized Rayleigh quotient $\gamma_{rk}^{AR}$, so $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \mathbf{u}_{rk}^* = \mathcal{O}(\epsilon') \approx \mathbf{0}$ (as well as $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial c_k = 0$ and $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \rho_k = \mathcal{O}(\epsilon')$ with $\epsilon' = 10^{-9}$). However, when backpropagating the outer training loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{train}}$ in Equation (16) with respect to the neural network parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ *across* multiple unfolded layers $\ell = 1, \dots, L$, the intermediate beam update $\mathbf{W}_i^{(\ell+1)}$ in Equation (14) depends explicitly on $\mathbf{B}_i^{(\ell)}$ and $\mathbf{Q}_i^{(\ell)}$, which are not at stationary points with respect to $\mathbf{u}_{rk}^{(\ell)}, c_k^{(\ell)},$ and $\rho_k^{(\ell)}$. Consequently, differentiating through the layer-wise recomputation mappings $\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)} \mapsto \boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)} \mapsto \mathbf{W}^{(\ell+1)}$ introduces second-order Jacobian terms of the MMSE combiner and equalizer updates rather than treating $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ as fixed coordination inputs. Explicitly distinguishing between the single-layer first-order Wirtinger gradient test in Equation (12) (where $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$ is stationary up to $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon')$ because it is stream-specific) and the multi-layer computational graph used during outer backpropagation for $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_i}\mathcal{L}_{\text{train}}$ would make the backpropagation structure and unit-test equivalence completely transparent.

2. **Order of Operations in the Unfolded WMMSE Update: Cluster/Association Masking, Power-Price Bisection $\mu_i$, and Multiplicative Regularization $\lambda_{\text{reg}}$ (Section V-D, Eq. 14):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section V-D (pages 4–5), each node updates its beam matrix via Equation (14):
     $$\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i = \mathbf{W}_i + \eta^{(\ell)}\Big((\mathbf{Q}_i + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_i - \mathbf{W}_i\Big), \quad \text{with } \mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i - \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*}\mathcal{L},$$
     where $\lambda = \lambda_{\text{reg}}\mu_i$, and $\mu_i \ge 0$ is the smallest scalar satisfying $\|(\mathbf{Q}_i + \mu \mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_i\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2 \le P_i^{\max}$ (found by bisection and treated as a constant in backpropagation). The text states that this step *"is followed by the relaxation $\mathbf{W}_i^{(\ell+1)} = (1 - \delta^{(\ell)})\mathbf{W}_i^{(\ell)} + \delta^{(\ell)}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i$, the cluster or association mask, and a scaling onto the power budget."* Three coupled mathematical issues in this sequence warrant clarification:
     1. *Non-commutativity of the cluster/association mask and the power-price bisection:* For an AP $m$ and an unserved stream $j$ where $c_{mj} = 0$ (so $\mathbf{w}_{mj} = \mathbf{0}$), inspecting Equation (12) shows that the gradient column $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}_{mj}^*}\mathcal{L}$—and therefore the $j$-th column of $\mathbf{B}_m = \mathbf{Q}_m\mathbf{W}_m - \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_m^*}\mathcal{L}$—is generally **non-zero** because the effective gains $S_{kj}^{(1)} = \sum_{m'} \mathbf{h}_{m'k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{m'j}$ and $S_{rkj}^{AR} = \mathbf{u}_{rk}^{\mathrm{H}}\sum_{m'} \mathbf{G}_{m'r}\mathbf{w}_{m'j}$ include transmissions from other APs $m' \neq m$ that do serve stream $j$ (and similarly at relay $r$ for $S_{kj}^{(2)}$ when $a_{rj} = 0$). If the cluster mask $c_{mj} \in \{0,1\}$ (or relay association mask $a_{rj} \in \{0,1\}$) is applied only *after* Equation (14) rather than zeroing out the unserved columns of $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*}\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathbf{B}_i$ *prior* to solving the bisection for $\mu_i$, then $\|(\mathbf{Q}_i + \mu \mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_i\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2$ counts phantom power allocated to unserved streams that are subsequently discarded by the post-relaxation mask. This artificially inflates the Lagrange multiplier $\mu_i$, leaves the node strictly below its power budget, and breaks exact equivalence to classical per-node WMMSE at $\eta^{(\ell)} = \delta^{(\ell)} = \lambda_{\text{reg}} = 1$.
     2. *Nullification of the learned regularization $\lambda_{\text{reg}}$ when the power constraint is inactive ($\mu_i = 0$):* Because the learned regularization enters strictly as a multiplicative factor $\lambda = \lambda_{\text{reg}}\mu_i$ rather than an independent additive Tikhonov regularizer, whenever the unconstrained (or $\lambda_E$-regularized) step lies inside the power ball ($\|\mathbf{Q}_i^{-1}\mathbf{B}_i\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2 \le P_i^{\max}$, which frequently occurs when the energy price $\lambda_E > 0$ already adds $\frac{\lambda_E}{\eta_A}\mathbf{I}$ inside $\mathbf{Q}_i$ in Equation 13), complementary slackness yields $\mu_i = 0$. Consequently, $\lambda = \lambda_{\text{reg}}\mu_i = 0$ identically regardless of the network's predicted $\lambda_{\text{reg}} \ge 0$, completely nullifying the learned regularizer and blocking gradient flow to the $\lambda_{\text{reg}}$ head output. (Note also that at $\ell = 0$ in Section V-C, initial beams are scaled to $\sqrt{s_{ik}P_i^{\max}}$, consuming the full power budget even when $\lambda_E > 0$).
     3. *Specification of the post-relaxation power scaling and parallel Jacobi structure:* When $\lambda_E > 0$, nodes intentionally transmit below full power $P_i^{\max}$ to save energy (down to $8.6\text{ W}$ at $\lambda_E = 0.5$ in Section VIII-I), whereas when $\eta^{(\ell)}\delta^{(\ell)} > 1$ or $\lambda_{\text{reg}} < 1$, the iterate can exceed $P_i^{\max}$. Furthermore, as the authors note, because no nonlinearity occurs between Equation (14) and the relaxation step, $\eta^{(\ell)} \in (0,2)$ and $\delta^{(\ell)} \in (0,1)$ act solely through their product $\eta^{(\ell)}\delta^{(\ell)} \in (0,2)$, creating a 1D non-identifiable manifold per layer in the $2L$ head outputs.
   - **Actionable Improvement:** Clarify in Section V-D that $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*}\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathbf{B}_i$ are masked to each node's served streams prior to solving the bisection for $\mu_i$, explicitly state that the post-relaxation power scaling is an upper-bound radial projection onto the ball $\mathbf{W}_i^{(\ell+1)} \leftarrow \mathbf{W}_i^{(\ell+1)} \min\!\big(1, \sqrt{P_i^{\max}} / \|\mathbf{W}_i^{(\ell+1)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\big)$ (rather than boundary normalization, which would force full power $P_i^{\max}$ even when $\lambda_E > 0$) and whether gradients propagate through the norm denominator during training, note that $\lambda_{\text{reg}}$ acts only when $\mu_i > 0$, and mention that the neutral update $\eta^{(\ell)}=\delta^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{\text{reg}}=1$ corresponds to a synchronous *parallel Jacobi* best-response WMMSE step across co-serving APs (which mathematically explains why the undamped update oscillates in Section VIII-B).

4. **Federated Protocol Specification: Self-Inclusion in Personalized Updates, Uplink Knapsack vs. Bidirectional Headroom Accounting, Cross-Node Signature Standardization, and Latent Alignment (Sections V-C, V-F, and V-G):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Several architectural and protocol details in Sections V-C, V-F, and V-G (Steps 3–4, 11, and 12–15, pages 4–6) are underspecified or introduce hidden centralized couplings:
     1. *Double-counting of local progress and $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i^{\text{rec}}$ tracking under $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\theta}_i + \rho_p \Delta\bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i$ (Section V-G):* During the local Adam steps of epoch $t$, node $i$ updates its backbone from $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i^{\text{rec}}$ to $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i = \boldsymbol{\theta}_i^{\text{rec}} + \Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$. It is also not specified whether reported cumulative FL bytes (e.g., Fig. 12b) count only uplink traffic $\sum_i q_i S^\uparrow$ or both uplink and downlink traffic.
     3. *Distributed computation of the standardized signature $\mathbf{s}_i(t)$ prior to upload (Section V-G):* Node $i$ locally computes its urgency $\Gamma_i^{\text{FL}}$—which depends on signature change $d_i^H = \|\mathbf{s}_i(t) - \mathbf{s}_i(t-1)\|$—*prior* to candidate selection and upload.      4. *Coordinate alignment of the shared backbone between unshared local adapters/heads and centralized backpropagation coupling (Sections V-C and V-F):* Because only the intermediate backbone $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ ($32 \to 64 \to 64 \to 32$) is federated while the role-specific two-layer ReLU input adapters and linear output heads remain strictly local, averaging $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ across nodes (including across APs and relays with cross-role weight $\kappa_{AR} = 0.3$) requires the 32-dimensional latent coordinates of different nodes' adapters and heads to stay aligned. 2. **Confounding of Objective Mismatch ($\rho = 0.98$ Outage Penalty and QoS Pricing) in Perfect-CSI Evaluations and Unisolated Robustness Mechanisms Under Channel Aging (Sections VI, VII, VIII-A, VIII-B, VIII-E, VIII-K, and IX; Tables II–III; Fig. 8)**
   - **Observation:** Section VII states that "CSI is perfect except in the aging sweep... Training always includes the robust outage term with $\rho = 0.98$, also in the perfect-CSI experiments." Meanwhile, Section IV-A and Section VI note that all optimization baselines (`Centralized WMMSE-SCA/AO`, `Local-WMMSE`, and `Exact MINLP`) maximize pure unconstrained PF utility without the QoS outage constraint (9f) and treat $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ as exact (certainty equivalence).    - **Potential Concern:** This setup creates four analytical confounds across the perfect-CSI and stale-CSI evaluations:
     1. *Perfect-CSI utility penalty:* In the perfect-CSI experiments ($\rho = 1.0$, Sections VIII-A–D and VIII-F), training `RAP-FedWMMSE` with synthetic CSI errors at $\rho = 0.98$ ($\lambda_O = 1$) and primal–dual QoS/DF pricing ($\eta_Q = \eta_D = 0.2$) pulls power away from strong users toward boundary users to protect against channel estimation errors that are absent at evaluation time.
