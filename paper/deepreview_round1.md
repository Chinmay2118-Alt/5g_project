# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY

## Summary
This paper proposes RAP-FedWMMSE, a model-driven distributed beam optimization framework for intelligent-relay-assisted cell-free massive MIMO networks operating under blockage and fronthaul constraints. Operating on two timescales, the algorithm pairs slow-timescale capacitated binary matching for relay-user association and adaptive half-duplex phase splitting with fast-timescale unfolded per-node regularized WMMSE layers parameterized by role-specific neural adapters. To handle quality-of-service (QoS) outage targets and the non-smooth two-hop decode-and-forward (DF) rate bottleneck without labeled datasets, the authors introduce in-layer primal-dual corrections with stop-gradient DF bottleneck pricing, self-supervised CSI-error sampling, and an event-triggered, knapsack-budgeted personalized federated learning protocol evaluated across 14 experiments, including certified global upper bounds via spatial branch-and-bound.

## Strengths
* **Principled Adaptation of Unfolded Distributed WMMSE to Two-Phase Relaying:** The paper thoughtfully extends per-node WMMSE unfolding to a heterogeneous two-phase half-duplex DF relay architecture by introducing role-specific neural adapters (distinguishing access points from relays) and neutral initialization that exactly recovers the classical per-node WMMSE step.
* **Effective Stop-Gradient Primal-Dual Pricing and Robust Self-Supervised Training:** Formulating the DF relay-bottleneck dual price using a stop-gradient operator (`sg(C_k^D) - C_{rk}^{AR}`) alongside a smoothed soft-min surrogate prevents gradient updates from artificially depressing the destination rate to satisfy the relay bottleneck, while Monte Carlo CSI-error sampling allows the method to outperform centralized certainty-equivalent optimization under stale CSI ($\rho \le 0.9$).
* **Rigorous Evaluation Protocol and Transparent Reporting of Negative Findings:** The authors evaluate all baselines fairly (giving the no-relay benchmark the full frame $\tau=1$), benchmark against certified global upper bounds using SCIP spatial branch-and-bound on a small network, and candidly report negative outcomes—including that federated aggregation provides no utility gain over local training in their data-rich simulated regime, cooperative multi-relay forwarding ($L_{\max}=2$) lowers utility, and the trained parameters degrade on degenerate single-antenna topologies.

## Weaknesses
* **Conflation of Slow and Fast Coordination Vectors Under $\zeta$:** The manuscript overloads the symbol $\zeta$ to represent two distinct mechanisms: a slow-timescale real-valued feature vector of $4K + RK$ scalars updated once per epoch for neural hyperparameter prediction (Eq. 10), and a fast-timescale "gradient-exact" message of $2K^2 + 4K + R(K^2 + 2K + KN_R)$ complex values (352 complex numbers per layer) exchanged at every unfolded layer inside a coherence block to compute exact network gradients. Please consider introducing distinct symbols for these slow and fast payloads and explicitly detailing the per-layer exchanges in Algorithm 1.
* **Asymmetric Runtime Baseline and Omission of Fast Distributed/Unfolded WMMSE Baselines:** The claimed $50\times$ inference speedup ($\approx 0.34\text{ s}$ vs. $\approx 20\text{ s}$) is largely an implementation artifact of comparing vectorized closed-form layer updates against a centralized successive convex approximation (SCA) loop that invokes a general-purpose conic interior-point solver (Clarabel via CVXPY) at every iteration. The authors might consider contextualizing this solver asymmetry and comparing against modern closed-form or distributed unfolded WMMSE baselines that avoid CVXPY parsing overhead (Reference Paper: Coordinated Sum-Rate Maximization in Multicell MU-MIMO With Deep Unrolling, Lukas Schynol and Marius Pesavento, 2023; Reference Paper: Deep Graph Unfolding for QoS-Aware Distributed Beamforming in Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Tianwei Yang, Cheng Zhang, Wen Wang, Chang-Wei Zhang, and Yong-Ming Huang, 2026; Reference Paper: Distributed Multiuser Precoding via Unfolding WMMSE for Multicell MIMO Downlinks, Chenyang Yi, Wei Xu, Yao Sun, Derrick Wing Kwan Ng, and Zhaocheng Wang, 2025).
* **Confounded Attribution of Utility Gains Across Coordination, Phase-Split Adaptation, and Learned Parameters:** In the main comparisons, the non-learned distributed baselines (`Local-WMMSE`, `RZF`, `MRT`) are simultaneously restricted to zero inter-node coordination and a suboptimal fixed phase split ($\tau=0.5$, which alone sacrifices $\approx 3$ PF utility units), while `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` in Table II ($3.87$ PF utility) already achieves a $+7.30$ gain over `Local-WMMSE` ($-3.43$) via $\zeta$ coordination before any neural learning or $\tau$ adaptation is applied. Please consider explicitly decomposing the $+11.3$ margin into coordination, slow-timescale $\tau$ adaptation, and isolated neural parameter learning ($\approx +0.9\text{--}1.0$ at $L=6$ in Fig. 9), and updating the Abstract's claim regarding the best non-learned distributed baseline accordingly.
* **Incremental Practical Value of the Federated Learning Pipeline and Centralized Backpropagation Shortcut:** While combining event triggering, knapsack client selection under residual fronthaul headroom, top-$k$ sparsification, and kernel-weighted personalization reduces FL communication by 45% over FedAvg, federated aggregation yields zero measurable PF utility improvement over purely local training ($-0.06 \pm 0.19$) because each node trains on unlimited synthetic channels and uses a simulation-only cross-node backpropagation shortcut across all nodes' layers (Reference Paper: Client Selection for Wireless Federated Learning With Data and Latency Heterogeneity, Xiaobing Chen, Xiangwei Zhou, Hongchao Zhang, Mingxuan Sun, and H. Vincent Poor, 2024; Reference Paper: Towards Personalized Federated Learning, Alysa Ziying Tan, Han Yu, Lizhen Cui, and Qiang Yang, 2023; Reference Paper: Sparse Communication for Distributed Gradient Descent, Alham Fikri Aji and Kenneth Heafield, 2017).
* **Omissions in Mathematical Formulations and Reproducibility Details:** Several mathematical definitions and architectural hyperparameters needed to make the paper self-contained—such as the exact analytical expressions for the per-node curvature $\mathbf{Q}_i$ and gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$ under the soft-min and stop-gradient objective, neural network dimensions and parameter counts $\dim(\theta_i)$, calibrated matching and urgency weights $(\alpha_{1:4}, \beta_{1:4})$, and the formal indicator for active relay circuit power in MINLP $\mathbf{P0}$—are omitted from the text.

### [System Model & MINLP Problem Formulation] (Pages: 2-3)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Timescale Coupling, Feasibility, and Probability Measure in Problem Formulation $\mathbf{P0}$ (Section III-C, Eq. 8; Section IV, Eqs. 9a–9f):**
   Problem $\mathbf{P0}$ in Equations (9a)–(9f) is defined as *"the design problem over one coherence block"* with respect to the decision variables $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{A}, \tau)$. However, a potential concern is that two of its constraints operate on the slow (per-epoch) timescale or rely on variables and probability spaces outside the single-block decision set:
   - **Coupling of Per-Epoch Fronthaul Constraints (Eq. 8 in Eq. 9f):** Constraint (9f) enforces the per-epoch fronthaul inequalities from Equation (8), $D_m^{\text{data}} + D_m^{\text{FL}} \le F_m^{\max} T_e$ and $D_r^{\text{FL}} \le F_r^{\text{ctrl}} T_e$, where $D_m^{\text{data}} = B T_e \sum_k c_{mk} \bar{r}_k$ depends on the epoch's mean user rates $\bar{r}_k$ over epoch duration $T_e$ rather than the single-block instantaneous rates $r_k$, and the federated learning (FL) payload volumes $D_m^{\text{FL}}, D_r^{\text{FL}}$ are governed by slow-timescale client selection variables (Section V-G) that are not defined in Section III-C or included in the decision variables of $\mathbf{P0}$. Furthermore, enforcing $D_m^{\text{data}} + D_m^{\text{FL}} \le F_m^{\max} T_e$ as a hard constraint inside the physical-layer optimization $\mathbf{P0}$ mathematically imposes an upper cap on the achievable user sum rate $\sum_k c_{mk} \bar{r}_k \le F_m^{\max} / B$ at each AP. Later sections (Section V-G and Section VIII-I) clarify that APs whose user data alone exceeds $F_m^{\max} T_e$ are permitted to transmit user data at those rates and are simply excluded from sending FL traffic ($D_m^{\text{FL}} = 0$). As written in (8) and (9f), however, such high-rate beamforming solutions would violate (9f) and render $\mathbf{P0}$ infeasible.
   - **Undefined Probability Space in Outage Constraint (Eq. 9e):** For a single coherence block with given channel realizations $\mathbf{h}_{mk}, \mathbf{G}_{mr}, \mathbf{g}_{rk}$, the rate $r_k$ is a deterministic function of $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{A}, \tau)$. Including $\Pr\{r_k < R^{\min}\} \le \epsilon_k\ \forall k$ inside the single-block formulation $\mathbf{P0}$ leaves the underlying probability measure unspecified in Sections III and IV—specifically, whether $\Pr\{\cdot\}$ is evaluated over the conditional distribution of CSI estimation errors given an imperfect channel estimate $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ within a block (as in the error model $\mathbf{H} = \rho\hat{\mathbf{H}} + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\mathbf{E}$ introduced later in Section V-E), or over the ergodic distribution of channel realizations and user drops across an epoch (as evaluated under perfect CSI in Section VIII-A). Moreover, because the optimization baselines solve $\mathbf{P0}$ with (9e) omitted while the proposed method incorporates dual prices $\lambda_k^Q$ and an outage penalty during training, the methods optimize slightly different objective/constraint trade-offs whenever $\lambda_k^Q > 0$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly separate fast-timescale (per-block) variables from slow-timescale (per-epoch) variables in Section IV, define the probability measure underlying $\Pr\{r_k < R^{\min}\} \le \epsilon_k$ in (9e), and clarify that (8) constrains the available per-epoch FL participation headroom $D_m^{\text{FL}} \le [F_m^{\max} T_e - D_m^{\text{data}}]_+$ during client selection rather than capping physical-layer user data rates in $\mathbf{P0}$.

2. **Formal Specification of Active Relays and Duty-Cycle Weighting in the Power Consumption Model (Section III-C, Eq. 7; Section IV, Eqs. 9a–9b):**
   - **Mathematical Definition of "$\text{r active}$":** In Equation (7), the relay power consumption sum is indexed over "$r\text{ active}$", adding a fixed circuit power $P_R^c > 0$ per active relay:
     $$P_{\text{tot}} = \sum_m \left(\frac{1}{\eta_A}\sum_k \|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2 + P_A^c\right) + \sum_{r\text{ active}} \left(\frac{1}{\eta_R}\sum_k \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2 + P_R^c\right).$$
     Because $-\lambda_E P_{\text{tot}}$ appears directly in the objective of the MINLP $\mathbf{P0}$ (Eq. 9a) where the binary association $\mathbf{A} = [a_{rk}]$ and relay beams $\mathbf{V} = \{\mathbf{v}_{rk}\}$ are optimization variables, leaving the condition "$r\text{ active}$" informal creates a potential concern regarding how $P_R^c$ depends on the decision variables—specifically, whether activity is determined on the slow timescale by the binary association indicator $\mathbb{I}\{\sum_k a_{rk} \ge 1\} = \max_k a_{rk}$, or on the fast timescale by nonzero transmit power $\mathbb{I}\{\sum_k \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2 > 0\}$. Expressing this indicator explicitly (for example, multiplying $P_R^c$ by $\max_k a_{rk}$ or introducing an auxiliary binary variable $z_r \in \{0,1\}$ with $z_r \ge a_{rk}\ \forall k$) is needed to fully specify the mixed-integer structure of $\mathbf{P0}$ when $\lambda_E > 0$ and to clarify whether $P_R^c$ enters the relay association step (Section V-A) or exact branch-and-bound benchmarks.
   - **Half-Duplex Phase Duty-Cycle Factors:** Section III-A specifies that the unit-length coherence block is split into Phase I of duration $\tau$ (where APs transmit and relays listen) and Phase II of duration $1-\tau$ (where relays transmit and APs are silent). However, Equation (7) sums the AP RF power $\frac{1}{\eta_A}\sum_k \|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2$ and relay RF power $\frac{1}{\eta_R}\sum_k \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2$ without weighting them by their active transmission durations $\tau$ and $1-\tau$. If $\sum_k \|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2 \le P_m^{\max}$ and $\sum_k \|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2 \le P_r^{\max}$ in (9b) denote instantaneous per-phase transmit power limits during Phase I and Phase II, the average RF power over the coherence block would scale these terms by $\tau$ and $1-\tau$, respectively; otherwise, the text should clarify whether the beam norms in (7) and (9b) already absorb the duty cycle.

3. **Physical-Layer Coding, Synchronization, and Bottleneck Assumptions in Two-Phase Decode-and-Forward Rates (Section III-B, Eqs. 1–6):**
   Several physical-layer and information-theoretic modeling assumptions in Equations (1)–(6) warrant explicit clarification in Section III-B:
   - **Parallel-Channel Rate Summation Across Phases (Eq. 4):** The destination capacity is modeled as the sum of Phase I and Phase II spectral efficiencies, $C_k^D = \tau \log_2(1 + \gamma_k^{(1)}) + (1 - \tau) \log_2(1 + \gamma_k^{(2)})$. Information-theoretically, achieving the sum of mutual informations across two half-duplex phases of arbitrary durations $\tau$ and $1-\tau$ requires the relays to re-encode the decoded message using an independent Gaussian codebook (parallel-channel coding / incremental redundancy across phases), rather than transmitting the identical modulated codeword (repetition coding, which requires equal phase durations and sums SINRs inside a single logarithm). Explicitly stating the independent codebook assumption in Section III-A/B would clarify the basis for Equation (4).
   - **Coherent Multi-Relay Combining in Phase II (Eqs. 1 and 3):** Both the Phase I SINR $\gamma_k^{(1)}$ in (1) and the Phase II SINR $\gamma_k^{(2)}$ in (3) assume coherent over-the-air amplitude-and-phase superposition at single-antenna UE $k$ across geographically distributed transmitters—specifically, $\left|\sum_r a_{rk} \mathbf{g}_{rk}^{\text{H}} \mathbf{v}_{rk}\right|^2$ and $\sum_{j\neq k}\left|\sum_r \mathbf{g}_{rk}^{\text{H}} \mathbf{v}_{rj}\right|^2$ across cooperating relays when $L_{\max} > 1$. Achieving coherent phase alignment in Phase II requires tight carrier-phase and symbol-timing synchronization across the assisting relays as well as joint Phase II CSI, which should be stated explicitly as a modeling assumption given that each relay only has a low-rate control link (Section III-C).
   - **Strict Full-Message Decoding Bottleneck (Eq. 6):** Equation (6) defines the achievable rate of an assisted UE as $r_k = \min\big\{C_k^D, \min_{r: a_{rk}=1} C_{rk}^{AR}\big\}$, whereas an unassisted UE achieves $\tau \log_2(1 + \gamma_k^{(1)})$. Because the minimum is enforced unconditionally over all assigned relays, activating any relay whose AP-to-relay capacity $C_{rk}^{AR}$ is weaker than the Phase I direct link $\tau \log_2(1 + \gamma_k^{(1)})$—or adding a second assisting relay ($L_{\max} > 1$) with a weaker $C_{rk}^{AR}$—strictly reduces $r_k$ below the rate achievable without that relay. Briefly noting in Section III-B that (6) assumes a strict full-message decoding protocol at every assigned relay (without rate-splitting or opportunistic relay muting) would clarify the coding model early on and contextualize the performance degradation later observed for $L_{\max} = 2$ in Section VIII-C.

### [Proposed Algorithm: RAP-FedWMMSE Architecture & Training] (Pages: 3-4)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Conflation of the Slow Neural-Input Vector $\zeta(t)$ and the Fast Per-Layer Gradient-Exact Coordination Payload Under the Symbol $\zeta$ (Section V-C, V-D, V-H, and Algorithm 1)**
   - **Potential Concern:** There is an ambiguity and apparent contradiction in the notation, dimensionality, and operational timescale of the coordination vector $\zeta$:
     - In **Section V-C (Eq. 10)** and **Algorithm 1 (Line 9)**, $\zeta(t) = [\boldsymbol{\lambda}^Q, \boldsymbol{\nu}, \mathbf{I}^{(1)}, \mathbf{I}^{(2)}, \bar{\mathbf{r}}]$ is explicitly defined as a *slow* coordination vector refreshed once per epoch, consisting of $4K + RK$ real scalar numbers ($4(8) + 2(8) = 48$ real numbers under the default parameters $K=8, R=2$). This vector serves as a slow context feature fed into the neural adapter and backbone $\theta_i$ to predict the unfolded layer hyperparameters.
     - In **Section V-D**, however, the fast per-coherence-block, per-layer beam update in Eq. (11) requires the exact network objective gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$, which the text states is *"computable from local CSI plus the per-stream aggregate gains, equalizers, weights and combiners carried by $\zeta$."* Because MMSE receive combiners $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$, equalizers, and weights $\rho = 1/\varepsilon$ are recomputed inside *every unfolded layer* $\ell = 1, \dots, L$ on the fast timescale, these intermediate quantities cannot be supplied by the static epoch-level vector $\zeta(t)$ from Eq. (10).
     - Confirming this distinction, **Section V-H (Signaling overhead)** states that *"One exchange of the gradient-exact $\zeta$ carries $2K^2 + 4K$ complex numbers for the UE links plus $R(K^2 + 2K + KN_R)$ for the relays — 352 complex values per layer at the default size"* (totaling $352 \times 6 = 2,112$ complex values per coherence block at $L=6$).
   - **Impact on Clarity & Reproducibility:** Overloading $\zeta$ to represent both the 48-scalar slow neural input summary (updated once per epoch) and the 352-complex-value fast message-passing payload (exchanged at each of the $L$ layers within every coherence block) obscures the two-timescale communication protocol. Furthermore, Algorithm 1 (Line 5 and the *"Deployment (per block)"* block) depicts nodes refreshing $\zeta(t)$ once per epoch and then running $L$ layers on local CSI plus $\zeta$, omitting the $L$ fast inter-node communication rounds required per coherence block (and leaving unclear whether this $352L$-complex-value per-block signaling is deducted from the fronthaul/control budgets in Eq. 8). Introducing distinct notation—such as $\zeta_{\text{slow}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4K+RK}$ for the epoch-level neural feature vector in Eq. (10) and $\zeta_{\text{fast}}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{C}^{2K^2+4K+R(K^2+2K+KN_R)}$ for the per-layer WMMSE message passing—explicitly listing the mathematical quantities packed into the $352$ complex values, and showing the per-layer exchange inside the layer loop of Algorithm 1 would resolve this ambiguity.

2. **Reconciliation of the Classical Per-Node WMMSE Surrogate Update with the Exact Augmented Objective Gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$ (Section V-D, Eq. 11, and Section V-E, Eq. 12)**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section V-D, the unfolded beam update at node $i$ is given by Eq. (11):
     $$\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i = \mathbf{W}_i + \eta^{(\ell)}\left((\mathbf{Q}_i + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_i - \mathbf{W}_i\right), \quad \text{with } \mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i - \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J.$$
     The text asserts two simultaneous properties: (i) $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$ is *"the exact gradient of the network objective,"* and (ii) *"[w]ith $\eta^{(\ell)} = \lambda_{\text{reg}} = 1$ and $\delta^{(\ell)} = 0$, (11) is exactly the classical per-node WMMSE update; a unit test verifies both this neutrality and the gradient's agreement with autograd on the global objective to machine precision."* Several omitted mathematical definitions and sign conventions require reconciliation for a reader to verify and reproduce this equivalence:
     - **Sign Convention and Surrogate Cancellation:** At $\eta^{(\ell)} = 1$, Eq. (11) simplifies to $\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i = (\mathbf{Q}_i + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i - \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J)$. First, in Eq. (12), $J$ is written as a utility to be *maximized* (positive $\omega_k \ln(r_k + \epsilon_0)$ and negative $-\lambda_E P_{\text{tot}}$). A regularized Newton ascent step on a concave quadratic surrogate $J(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i) \approx J(\mathbf{W}_i) + 2\operatorname{Re}\langle \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J, \widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i - \mathbf{W}_i \rangle - \langle \widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i - \mathbf{W}_i, \mathbf{Q}_i(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_i - \mathbf{W}_i)\rangle$ yields the numerator $\mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i + \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$, whereas the minus sign in $\mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i - \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$ corresponds to minimizing the negated loss $\mathcal{L} = -J$. Second, in classical WMMSE (holding MMSE combiners $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$, equalizers, and MSE weights $\rho = 1/\varepsilon$ fixed), setting $\mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i - \nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} \mathcal{L}$ cancels the quadratic term $\mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{W}_i$ to recover the classical linear effective-channel numerator *only* if $\mathbf{Q}_i$ and the per-phase MSE weights $\rho$ are scaled by the exact outer chain-rule derivatives of Eq. (12). Because $J$ in Eq. (12) replaces the hard minimum in Eq. (6) with a smooth soft-min of sharpness $\kappa$, includes a rate floor $\epsilon_0$ inside $\ln(r_k + \epsilon_0)$, adds QoS dual terms $\lambda_k^Q(r_k - R^{\min})$ and asymmetric stop-gradient DF dual terms $-\nu_{rk}(\text{sg}(C_k^D) - C_{rk}^{AR})$, and subtracts $\lambda_E P_{\text{tot}}$, the local curvature $\mathbf{Q}_i$ must incorporate the soft-min probability weights, $\frac{\omega_k}{r_k + \epsilon_0} + \lambda_k^Q$, $+\nu_{rk}$ (on $C_{rk}^{AR}$ only, since $\text{sg}(C_k^D)$ zeros out the $\nu_{rk}$ gradient contribution on $C_k^D$), and the energy penalty $\frac{\lambda_E}{\eta_i}\mathbf{I}$. Because explicit analytical formulas for $\mathbf{Q}_i$ and $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_i^*} J$ (separately for an AP node $i=m$ serving direct and relay links in Phase I, and a relay node $i=r$ transmitting in Phase II) are omitted, a reader cannot verify how $\mathbf{Q}_i$ is constructed to guarantee exact cancellation.
     - **Interaction of Soft-Min Routing with the Stop-Gradient DF Penalty:** In Eq. (12), the stop-gradient penalty $-\sum_{r,k}\nu_{rk}(\text{sg}(C_k^D) - C_{rk}^{AR})$ is introduced *"so the DF price raises the relay hop without depressing the destination rate."* However, AP Phase I precoders $\mathbf{w}_{mk}$ simultaneously determine both $C_k^D$ and $C_{rk}^{AR}$, so adding $+\nu_{rk} C_{rk}^{AR}$ still pulls Phase I AP power and beam directions toward the relay channels $\mathbf{G}_{mr}$, indirectly reducing $C_k^D$. Moreover, since Section V-D replaces the hard min in Eq. (6) with a soft-min of sharpness $\kappa$ inside $r_k$, the gradients of $\omega_k \ln(r_k + \epsilon_0)$ and $\lambda_k^Q r_k$ already route smoothly toward $C_{rk}^{AR}$ whenever the relay hop is the bottleneck; clarifying how soft-min gradient routing and the explicit $\nu_{rk}$ term interact would strengthen the design justification.
     - **Simultaneous Bisection for $\mu_i$ and Euclidean Projection:** Section V-D states that $\lambda = \lambda_{\text{reg}}\mu_i$ scales the classical WMMSE power price $\mu_i$ found by bisection, *and* that the damped step is followed by an exact Euclidean projection onto the node's power budget.
