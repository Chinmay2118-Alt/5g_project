# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper introduces RAP-FedWMMSE, a model-driven, two-timescale distributed beamforming and resource allocation framework for half-duplex decode-and-forward (DF) relay-assisted cell-free massive MIMO networks with severe direct-link blockage. On a slow timescale, the algorithm performs exact binary capacitated relay–user matching using large-scale channel gains and adapts the two-phase time split ($\tau$) via a 1D grid search. On a fast timescale, access points (APs) and relays execute unfolded per-node Weighted Minimum Mean-Square Error (WMMSE) layers driven by role-specific neural adapters/heads and a shared personalized federated backbone, augmented with in-layer primal–dual Quality-of-Service (QoS) and DF bottleneck pricing. Through extensive simulations across 14 experiments, including paired ablations and comparisons against a certified global Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) upper bound via SCIP on a small network, the authors demonstrate substantial tail-rate improvements for blocked users and transparent decompositions of where the performance gains originate.

## Strengths
* **Domain-Specific Adaptation of Distributed Unfolded WMMSE to Two-Phase DF Relaying:** The paper presents a well-crafted architectural synthesis that extends distributed per-node WMMSE deep unfolding to a two-phase half-duplex DF relay setting. Using a differentiable log-sum-exp soft-min surrogate alongside asymmetric stop-gradient dual pricing ($\nu_{rk}$) inside the unfolded layers effectively steers Phase I AP power toward binding relay hops without intentionally degrading direct destination capacity, while guaranteeing exact fallback to classical per-node WMMSE updates under neutral parameters.
* **Rigorous Experimental Methodology, Certified Bounds, and Exemplary Scientific Transparency:** The evaluation stands out for its statistical rigor and candor, featuring paired comparisons across 100 drops on 10 deployments, exact global optimality gap verification via spatial branch-and-bound in SCIP, and an honest reporting of negative results (such as the lack of utility gain from federated aggregation, utility degradation under multi-relay cooperation $L_{\max}=2$, and user starvation on single-antenna topologies).
* **Effective Two-Timescale System Decomposition for Blocked Cell-Free Users:** The overall hybrid algorithm achieves 76% of the centralized WMMSE-SCA benchmark's mean rate, triples the 5th-percentile cell-edge rate compared to relay-free centralized transmission (from 0.18 to 0.57 bit/s/Hz), and improves proportional-fair (PF) utility by $+11.31 \pm 0.43$ nats over uncoordinated Local-WMMSE at an order-of-magnitude lower computational runtime.

## Weaknesses
* **Modest Contribution of Neural Unfolding and Incremental Novelty Over Prior Distributed Unrolling:** The core algorithmic building blocks closely follow established distributed WMMSE unfolding and primal–dual QoS unrolling frameworks, and the authors' paired decomposition (Table III) shows that learning the layer parameters contributes only $+0.89 \pm 0.16$ nats out of the $+11.31$ nats total gain over Local-WMMSE, whereas non-learned fast coordination exchanges ($+7.30$) and slow-timescale phase-split/association adaptation ($+3.12$) capture over 92% of the benefit. Please consider also evaluating the untrained unfolded baseline with the training-free slow-timescale $\tau$ adaptation enabled and undamped classical steps ($\delta^{(\ell)}=1$) to cleanly isolate the neural contribution (Reference Paper: Coordinated Sum-Rate Maximization in Multicell MU-MIMO With Deep Unrolling, Lukas Schynol and Marius Pesavento, 2023; Reference Paper: Deep Graph Unfolding for QoS-Aware Distributed Beamforming in Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Tianwei Yang et al., 2026).
* **Centralized Backpropagation Coupling and Confounded Federated Learning Regime:** During self-supervised training, gradients are computed via global backpropagation across all nodes' layers on a shared batch (without counting the required synchronous backward coordination traffic against the fronthaul budget), and user-indexed slow dual prices in $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\mathrm{s}}(t)$ are carried across epochs despite drawing fresh user drops every epoch. Because even the `no-FL` baseline receives network-wide gradients and trains on unlimited simulated channel realizations, local training saturates on its own ($-0.06 \pm 0.19$ nats difference vs. personalized FL); the authors might consider detaching cross-node gradients for a strictly local training loss and restricting local dataset sizes to test federated generalization where its premise holds (Reference Paper: Federated Learning for Precoding Design in Cell-Free Massive MIMO Systems, Dong Wang et al., 2023; Reference Paper: Towards Personalized Federated Learning, Alysa Ziying Tan et al., 2023).
* **Asymmetric Channel-Aging Comparison and Omission of Statistically Coordinated Local-CSI Baselines:** In the stale-CSI evaluation (Fig. 8), RAP-FedWMMSE is retrained at each aging coefficient $\rho$ using $S=4$ error draws from the true error distribution while the centralized WMMSE-SCA benchmark remains certainty-equivalent, and the local-CSI comparisons omit statistically optimal distributed precoding such as Team MMSE, which is cited in Section II but excluded from Section VI. The authors might consider adding a sample-average robust centralized WMMSE-SCA baseline in Fig. 8 and comparing against or thoroughly contextualizing Team MMSE to clarify how much of the $+7.30$ fast-coordination gain could be achieved via statistical CSI sharing without exchanging $2,112$ complex scalars per block (Reference Paper: Team MMSE Precoding With Applications to Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Lorenzo Miretti, Emil Björnson, and David Gesbert, 2022; Reference Paper: Multipair DF Relaying With Network-Assisted Full-Duplex Cell-Free Massive MIMO, Tung Thanh Vu et al., 2023).
* **Unweighted Phase Durations in the Network Power Model:** Total network power $P_{\text{tot}}$ in Equation (7) sums Phase I AP transmit power and Phase II relay transmit power without weighting them by their orthogonal active durations $\tau$ and $1-\tau$. Because $P_{\text{tot}}$ is invariant to $\tau$, please consider weighting the per-phase transmit powers by $\tau$ and $1-\tau$ (or reporting true time-averaged power alongside $P_{\text{tot}}$) so that slow-timescale $\tau$ adaptation accounts for active AP vs. relay duty cycles when $\lambda_E > 0$ and energy comparisons against fixed-$\tau$ ($\tau=0.5$) and full-frame ($\tau=1$) baselines reflect true energy per coherence block.
* **Unverified Root Cause of Single-Antenna Starvation and Missing Implementation Equations:** On the small single-antenna validation network (Fig. 10), trained RAP-FedWMMSE underperforms its own untrained layers and nearly starves one user on 14% of drops, which is left to an unverified hypothesis. In addition, please consider explicitly providing the per-layer update equations for the unit-norm relay receive combiners $\mathbf{u}_{rk}$, MMSE equalizers, and MSE weights in Section V-D, and logging the predicted small-network parameters on the 14 starved drops to provide a verified diagnosis.

1. **Unqualified Claim of Overtaking the Centralized Benchmark Under Stale CSI (Abstract)**
   - **Verification & Context:** All quantitative claims and decompositions summarized in the Abstract and Section I—namely reaching $76\%$ of the centralized benchmark's mean rate ($3.49 / 4.57 = 76.37\%$, Table II), gaining $+11.3$ proportional-fair (PF) utility over uncoordinated Local-WMMSE ($7.88 - (-3.43) = +11.31$) decomposed into $+7.3$ from inter-node coordination, $+3.1$ from slow-timescale adaptation, and $+0.9$ from learned layer parameters (Table III), saving $22\%$ power ($23.9\text{ W}$ to $18.7\text{ W}$) for a $0.14$ utility loss (Section VIII-I), and reducing federated learning communication by $45\%$ ($11\text{ MB}$ vs. $20\text{ MB}$, Section VIII-F) relative to FedAvg with no measurable utility gain over local training (Section VIII-J)—are fully consistent with the technical sections and empirical tables later in the document.
   - **Potential Concern:** However, the Abstract states without qualification: *"The method overtakes the centralized relay-assisted benchmark under stale CSI..."* As explicitly documented later in Section VI, Section VIII-E, and Section VIII-K(viii), this comparison is asymmetric by design: RAP-FedWMMSE is retrained at each channel aging coefficient $\rho$ using a robust outage surrogate over $S=4$ error draws matching the evaluation error distribution (Section V-E), whereas the centralized WMMSE-SCA benchmark is evaluated as a certainty-equivalent optimizer that treats the aged channel estimate $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ as exact and has no robustness mechanism. Furthermore, the centralized no-relay benchmark remains higher in PF utility across all tested $\rho$ values. In Section VIII-E, the text notes that this experiment *"shows the value of robust training, not that the learned layers beat a robust centralized optimizer, which we did not implement,"* and in Section IX (Conclusion), the text qualifies the statement as overtaking *"certainty-equivalent centralized optimization under stale CSI."* Stating this claim in the Abstract without the *"certainty-equivalent"* qualifier is a potential concern because a reader of the Abstract alone may infer that distributed deep unfolding inherently outperforms centralized optimization under imperfect CSI, rather than reflecting the asymmetry between a robustly trained model and a non-robust baseline.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Qualify the statement in the Abstract to reflect the experimental setup accurately—for example: *"With robust training, the method overtakes the certainty-equivalent centralized relay-assisted benchmark under stale CSI..."*

2. **Characterization of the Unfolded Per-Node WMMSE Inputs and Fast Coordination Overhead in the Introduction (Section I)**
   - **Location:** Abstract, Page 1 (*"...centralized beam optimization requires full channel state information (CSI) at a central processor. We study RAP-FedWMMSE, a model-driven algorithm in which APs and half-duplex decode-and-forward relays optimize their beams from local CSI plus exchanged coordination quantities."*) and Section I, Page 1, second bullet (*"Unfolded per-node WMMSE layers: every AP and relay runs $L$ layers of a regularized WMMSE step whose step size, relaxation and regularization are predicted from local CSI and a slow coordination vector..."*).
   - **Potential Concern:** Two important structural nuances regarding the distributed coordination mechanism are omitted in the introductory summary:
     1. In Section I (second bullet), describing the unfolded per-node WMMSE layers as operating on *"local CSI and a slow coordination vector"* only describes the neural network forward pass that predicts the layer hyperparameters ($\eta^{(\ell)}, \delta^{(\ell)}, \lambda_{\text{reg}}$). The actual per-node WMMSE beam update in each of the $L$ layers requires a synchronous, per-layer fast coordination vector exchange $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ across the network to compute exact multi-node gradients (Section V-D).

As analyzed in Section V-H, at the default network size ($M=8, R=2, K=8, N_A=N_R=2, L=6$), exchanging $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ requires $2,112$ complex values per coherence block, which is nearly $10\times$ larger than uploading all raw instantaneous CSI to a central processor ($224$ complex values). Presenting the motivation in the Abstract and Introduction primarily around avoiding full CSI upload to a central processor without noting in Section I that each layer exchanges $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ (which only saves per-block signaling in the large-antenna-array regime) creates a slight scope mismatch with Section V-H.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** In the second bullet of Section I, explicitly mention that each unfolded layer exchanges a fast per-layer coordination vector $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\text{f}}^{(\ell)}$ (whose size is independent of the antenna counts $N_A, N_R$) to compute exact gradients, and briefly note that per-block signaling savings relative to centralized CSI upload apply in the large-array regime while the primary demonstrated benefits at small antenna counts are distributed computation and fast inference.

### [System Model & Problem Formulation] (Pages: 2-3)
#### Verification Summary

The physical-layer system model in Section III and the optimization problem formulation in Section IV were audited and verified for internal mathematical consistency. Specifically, the Phase I UE reception, relay-decoding, and Phase II relay-forwarding SINR expressions in Eqs. (1)–(3) and the two-phase decode-and-forward (DF) rate expressions in Eqs. (4)–(6) follow accurately from the three stated modeling assumptions: (i) independent Gaussian codebook re-encoding at the relays allowing mutual information accumulation across phases of durations $\tau$ and $1-\tau$, (ii) phase-synchronized coherent joint transmission across serving AP clusters in Phase I and assisting relays ($L_{\max} > 1$) in Phase II, and (iii) full-message decoding required at every assigned relay so the bottleneck minimum in Eq. (6) runs over all active relays $\{r : a_{rk} = 1\}$.

#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Unweighted Phase Durations in the Network Power Model ($P_{\text{tot}}$ in Section III-C, Eq. 7, and Problem P0 in Section IV-A, Eq. 8a):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section III-C, Eq. (7) defines the total network power consumption by directly summing the Phase I AP power and the Phase II active relay power without weighting them by their respective fractional phase durations $\tau$ and $1-\tau$:
     $$P_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{m}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_A}\sum_{k}\|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2 + P_A^c\right) + \sum_{r} b_r\left(\frac{1}{\eta_R}\sum_{k}\|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2 + P_R^c\right).$$
     Although Section III-C explicitly notes that $P_{\text{tot}}$ represents "the power drawn while transmitting rather than its time average," using this unweighted sum in the energy-penalized objective of Problem P0 (Eq. 8a) and in the energy-efficiency comparisons introduces three substantive physical and optimization considerations:
     1. *Physical and dimensional interpretation:* Under the half-duplex two-phase frame structure (Section III-A), APs transmit only during Phase I (duration $\tau$) and are silent during Phase II (duration $1-\tau$), while relays transmit only during Phase II. Because the two sets of transmitters are never active simultaneously, summing their active phase powers represents neither the instantaneous power drawn by the network at any point in the frame nor the time-averaged power (energy expended per unit-length coherence block), which for duty-cycled RF transmission is given by weighting the active transmit power terms by $\tau$ and $1-\tau$:
        $$\bar{P}_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = \sum_{m}\left(\frac{\tau}{\eta_A}\sum_{k}\|\mathbf{w}_{mk}\|^2 + P_A^c\right) + \sum_{r} b_r\left(\frac{1-\tau}{\eta_R}\sum_{k}\|\mathbf{v}_{rk}\|^2 + P_R^c\right)$$
        (or with phase-weighted active circuit powers if sleep modes are used during silent phases).
     2. *Distorted relative energy pricing and phase-split trade-off in Problem P0:* In Eq. (8a), $\tau$ is an optimization variable that scales the rate terms in Eqs. (4)–(5), whereas the energy penalty $-\lambda_E P_{\text{tot}}$ is completely invariant to $\tau$. Furthermore, penalizing $\lambda_E P_{\text{tot}}$ applies the same relative weight to AP and relay transmit powers regardless of $\tau$. When slow-timescale adaptation drives $\tau$ to its typical upper bound ($\tau = 0.8$), Phase II lasts only $20\%$ of the block ($1-\tau = 0.2$), yet Eq. (8a) penalizes a $1\text{ W}$ increase in relay RF power by $\lambda_E / \eta_R$—just as heavily as a $1\text{ W}$ increase in AP RF power that is active $4\times$ longer ($80\%$ of the frame).
     3. *Uneven baseline comparison against full-frame ($\tau=1$) operation:* When comparing two-phase relay-assisted methods ($\tau \in [0.2, 0.8]$) against the single-phase no-relay baseline ($\tau = 1$, where APs transmit for $100\%$ of the frame and $b_r = 0$), Eq. (7) counts both AP and relay RF powers as if both tiers transmitted for $100\%$ of the coherence block, overstating the physical transmit energy consumed by relay-assisted operation relative to the single-phase baseline.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Either weight the per-phase transmit power terms in Eq. (7) by $\tau$ and $1-\tau$ so that $P_{\text{tot}}$ measures true time-averaged power (energy per coherence block), or report time-averaged power alongside $P_{\text{tot}}$ and clarify in Sections III-C and IV-A why an unweighted sum across disjoint time phases was selected for the optimization penalty in Eq. (8a).

2. **Asymmetric Formulation and Enforcement of the Probabilistic QoS Constraint Across Methods (Section IV-A, Eq. 8f):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Problem P0 includes the conditional probabilistic QoS outage constraint $\Pr_{\mathbf{H}|\hat{\mathbf{H}}}\{r_k < R^{\min}\} \le \epsilon_k, \forall k$ in Eq. (8f), which reduces to the deterministic hard minimum-rate constraint $r_k \ge R^{\min}$ under perfect CSI (the default setting for all experiments except the channel-aging sweep). As stated in Section IV-A, RAP-FedWMMSE incorporates Eq. (8f) through dual prices $\lambda_k^Q$ (which enter both the unfolded layer objective in Eq. 14 and the Step 1 relay–user matching score $\Gamma_{rk}$ via $\alpha_4 \lambda_k^Q$) and an outage surrogate loss (Eq. 15), whereas all optimization baselines (Centralized WMMSE-SCA/AO, Centralized WMMSE no-relay, Local-WMMSE, and Exact MINLP in SCIP) solve P0 *without* constraint (8f) and with zero QoS prices ($\lambda_k^Q = 0$).
   - **Impact on Problem Consistency and Comparisons:**
     1. Because the optimization baselines maximize pure unconstrained proportional-fair (PF) utility $\sum_k \omega_k \ln(r_k + \epsilon_0) - \lambda_E P_{\text{tot}}$ while RAP-FedWMMSE optimizes a QoS-regularized objective and alters relay eligibility ($\bar{\beta}_{rk}^{RU} + \alpha_4 \lambda_k^Q > 0$) whenever any user's rate approaches or falls below $R^{\min} = 0.5\text{ bit/s/Hz}$, the methods are solving slightly different optimization problems. In particular, RAP-FedWMMSE actively sacrifices sum PF utility on stronger users to reduce QoS violations on borderline users, whereas the centralized benchmarks and the certified Exact MINLP upper bound do not.
     2. Under severe blockage ($25\text{ dB}$ additional attenuation) or low transmit power ($10\text{–}15\text{ dBm}$), enforcing Eq. (8f) as a strict constraint can make Problem P0 mathematically infeasible, which explains why no evaluated method enforces (8f) as a hard feasibility rule.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly distinguish in Section IV-A (and note in the limitations summary of Section VIII-K) between the unconstrained PF-utility problem solved by the optimization baselines/SCIP and the QoS-penalized relaxation solved by RAP-FedWMMSE, and clarify the extent to which active QoS pricing ($\lambda_k^Q > 0$) accounts for the trade-off between total PF utility and outage probability at the default operating point.

   - **Potential Concern:** Problem P0 in Eq. (8) is written as a single-block optimization over $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{A}, \tau)$ where the text below Eq. (8g) notes that decisions are taken on the channel estimate $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$. Two structural ambiguities arise in this presentation:
     1. In Eq.      2. While Eq.    - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly write $r_k(\hat{\mathbf{H}})$ in Eq. (8a) to make the conditioning on $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ consistent between Eqs. (8a) and (8f) (while also defining the aggregate channel notation $\mathbf{H} \triangleq \{\mathbf{h}_{mk}, \mathbf{G}_{mr}, \mathbf{g}_{rk}\}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$), and annotate the slow-timescale measurability of $(\mathbf{A}, \tau)$ directly alongside Problem P0.

4. **Physical Interpretation of Uncapped User Data Rates Under Fronthaul Overload and Coherent Multi-Node Transmission (Section III-B, Eq. 3, and Section IV-B, Eq. 9):**
   - **Potential Concern:**
     1. *Fronthaul overload vs. uncapped user data rates (Eq. 9):* In Section IV-B, AP $m$'s per-epoch user data volume is modeled as $D_m^{\text{data}} = B T_e \sum_k c_{mk} \bar{r}_k$ (reflecting full message replication of $\bar{r}_k$ across all $L_c$ serving APs in UE $k$'s cluster), and federated learning (FL) traffic is gated by $D_m^{\text{FL}} \le [F_m^{\max} T_e - D_m^{\text{data}}]_+$. The text notes that when $D_m^{\text{data}} > F_m^{\max} T_e$, the AP is counted as overloaded and sends no FL traffic, but user data is not throttled and reported utility is uncapped. If $F_m^{\max}$ represents the physical capacity of a shared transport link carrying both user data and FL traffic, operating with $D_m^{\text{data}} > F_m^{\max} T_e$ exceeds the physical link capacity for the user payload itself.
     2. *Phase synchronization across control-only relays ($L_{\max} > 1$ in Eq. 3):* Assumption (ii) in Section III-B states that for $L_{\max} > 1$, assisting relays in Phase II are phase-synchronized so their signals add coherently in Eq. However, Section IV-B models relays as having only a low-rate control link ($F_r^{\text{ctrl}}$) rather than a wired synchronization/data fronthaul, making symbol- and carrier-phase-synchronous joint transmission across distributed wireless relays a strong idealized assumption.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly note in Section IV-B that $D_m^{\text{data}} = B T_e \sum_k c_{mk} \bar{r}_k$ reflects full stream replication across all $L_c$ serving APs and clarify that keeping user rates uncapped when $D_m^{\text{data}} > F_m^{\max} T_e$ is a modeling choice designed to isolate FL congestion effects from data-plane throttling. 1. **Missing Specification of Relay Receive Combiner ($\mathbf{u}_{rk}$) Updates, MMSE Equalizers, and MSE Weights in Unfolded Layers (Section V-D & Algorithm 1, Pages 4–5):**

1. **Confounding of Slow-Timescale Adaptation, Primal–Dual Pricing, and Damped Relaxation Across Baselines and the Gain Decomposition (Section VI, pp. 5–6; Section VIII-A–B, pp. 6–7, Tables II–III; Section VIII-F, p. 8, Figs. 1–7, 9):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section VIII-B and Table III, the $+11.31 \pm 0.43$ proportional-fair (PF) utility margin of `RAP-FedWMMSE` ($7.88 \pm 0.51$) over `Local-WMMSE` ($-3.43 \pm 0.43$) is decomposed sequentially into three paired additions: (i) fast coordination $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\mathrm{f}}$ via `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` vs. `Local-WMMSE` ($+7.30 \pm 0.35$, reaching $3.87 \pm 0.46$), (ii) learned layer parameters at $L=6$ with $\tau=0.5$ and zero-price association held fixed ($+0.89 \pm 0.16$, reaching $4.76$ in Fig. 9), and (iii) slow-timescale adaptation ($+3.12 \pm 0.27$, rising from $4.76$ to $7.88$). While the numerical decomposition is internally consistent ($7.30 + 0.89 + 3.12 = 11.31$) and candidly reveals that over 92% of the total gain comes from non-learned components, three structural aspects of the baseline and ablation design introduce confounding factors:
     1. *Missing reverse ablation (untrained layers with slow-timescale adaptation enabled) and bundled slow-timescale mechanisms:* Because the ablation adds the learned parameters at a suboptimal fixed phase split ($\tau = 0.5$) before enabling slow-timescale adaptation (which moves the median $\tau$ to the upper bound of $0.8$), it does not report the utility of `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` when combined with the training-free slow-timescale protocol (Step 1 association and Step 2's 61-point $\tau$ grid search). Furthermore, the $+3.12 \pm 0.27$ step bundles three distinct mechanisms simultaneously: Step 2's $\tau$ grid search (which accounts for roughly $+3.0$ utility according to Fig. 7), Step 1's QoS-priced relay association, and the 3-epoch deployment warm-up of $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\mathrm{s}}$. Moreover, Section VI (p. 6) specifies that `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` uses "zero prices," leaving it ambiguous whether the trained curve in Fig. 9 ($+0.89 \pm 0.16$) includes the non-learned in-layer primal–dual updates ($\eta_Q = \eta_D = 0.2$, Step 8 of Algorithm 1) or if they are disabled alongside the slow prices.
     2. *Fixed $\tau = 0.5$ handicap on local and heuristic baselines:* In Table II and across the parameter sweeps in Figs. 1–6, `Local-WMMSE`, `RZF`, `MRT`, and `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` are locked to a fixed phase split $\tau = 0.5$, whereas `RAP-FedWMMSE` adapts $\tau \in [0.2, 0.8]$ via a 1D grid search over measured SINRs (converging to a median $\tau = 0.8$). As shown in Fig. 7, PF utility rises monotonically with $\tau$ over $[0.2, 0.8]$ for every relay-assisted method because Phase I carries most of the rate, so fixing $\tau = 0.5$ imposes a penalty of roughly $2.5\text{--}3.0$ PF utility units on those baselines on an axis unrelated to fast beam coordination.
     3. *Damped initialization ($\delta^{(\ell)} = 0.5$) vs. exact classical per-node WMMSE ($\delta^{(\ell)} = 1$):* Section V-D establishes that an unfolded layer reduces to the exact classical per-node WMMSE update when $\eta^{(\ell)} = \delta^{(\ell)} = \lambda_{\mathrm{reg}} = 1$. However, `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` in Section VI is configured with relaxation $\delta^{(\ell)} = 0.5$ (damping each layer step by half) at a short depth of $L=6$ layers, whereas `Local-WMMSE` runs for 100 iterations. At $L=6$, damping the update by $\delta^{(\ell)} = 0.5$ slows convergence relative to the undamped classical update ($\delta^{(\ell)} = 1$), which may inflate the $+0.89 \pm 0.16$ margin attributed to the learned parameters.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** To ensure hyperparameter parity and cleanly isolate the neural contribution, consider: (a) reporting `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` (as well as `Local-WMMSE` and `RZF`) *with* the training-free Step 2 $\tau$ grid search enabled in Table II and Section VIII-B; (b) reporting the exact classical coordinated per-node WMMSE update ($\eta^{(\ell)} = \delta^{(\ell)} = \lambda_{\mathrm{reg}} = 1$) at $L=6$ and at convergence ($L=100$) in Fig. 9; and (c) clarifying whether the in-layer dual updates ($\eta_Q = \eta_D = 0.2$) are active in Fig. 9 and adding a brief ablation setting $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^Q = \mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\nu} = \mathbf{0}$ with $\tau$ adaptation active to separate the effect of primal–dual pricing from the $\tau$ grid search.

3. **Unverified Root Cause of UE Starvation on the Single-Antenna Small Network (Section VI, p. 6; Section VIII-G, pp. 8–9, Fig. 10; Section VIII-K(iii), p. 10):**
   - **Potential Concern:** On the small single-antenna validation network ($M=2, R=1, K=2, N_A=N_R=1$) used to measure true optimality gaps against SCIP's certified spatial branch-and-bound upper bound (Fig. 10), trained `RAP-FedWMMSE` achieves a mean gap of $1.12$, underperforming its own `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` layers ($0.80$ gap) and nearly starving one UE on 14 of 100 drops. 4. **Shared-Batch Global Backpropagation and Confounded Experimental Regime in the Federated Learning Evaluation (Section V-F, p. 5; Section VII, p. 6; Section VIII-F, p. 8, Fig. 12; Section VIII-J, pp. 9–10, Fig. 14; Section VIII-K(i), p. 10):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section VIII-J (Fig. 14) and Section VIII-F (Fig. 12) transparently report a negative result: personalized federated aggregation yields no measurable utility improvement over non-federated local training (`no-FL`, paired difference $-0.06 \pm 0.19$ across 100 drops), leading the text to frame the value of Steps 12–15 as a 45% reduction in communication traffic relative to `FedAvg` ($11\text{ MB}$ vs. $20\text{ MB}$) for the same utility plateau. (9)). Because node $i$'s beam update at layer $\ell$ alters $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\mathrm{f}}^{(\ell+1)}$, which then passes through the unfolded updates of all other nodes $j \ne i$ in subsequent layers, end-to-end autograd provides every node with full network-wide Jacobian information—meaning all $M+R=10$ nodes within a deployment in the `no-FL` baseline already train synchronously on the same shared batches and receive global network-wide gradients.    - **Suggested Improvement:** Even within the current simulator, include a small diagnostic ablation in Section V-F and Section VIII-J where local data is restricted to a small finite buffer per node (e.g., 1–2 user drops or 8–16 blocks total), where cross-node gradient flow is detached during local updates (so each node treats incoming fast coordination vectors $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\mathrm{f}}^{(\ell)}$ as detached exogenous measurements and backpropagates solely through its own local per-layer beam updates in Eq. (13)), or where a newly arrived node is cold-started from federated weights, to test whether the role-aware personalized aggregation scheme ($\alpha_{ij}$, signature kernel $S_{ij}$, $\kappa_{AR}=0.3$) provides a generalization or sample-efficiency gain over `no-FL` when local training does not automatically saturate.

6. **Confounding of Per-Drop Warm-Up Overhead and Solver Formulation Disparity in Runtime Comparisons (Section VI, p. 5; Section VIII-H, pp. 7, 9, Table II, Fig. 11):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Table II and Fig. 11 compare the single-process wall-clock time of `RAP-FedWMMSE` ($\approx 0.34\text{ s}$ per drop at $K=8$) and `Unfolded WMMSE, untrained` ($\approx 0.09\text{ s}$) against `Centralized WMMSE-SCA/AO` ($\approx 20\text{ s}$ in Table II / Fig. 11, and $38\text{ s}$ in the main run).      1. *Warm-up blocks vs. single-block inference:* At deployment (Algorithm 1 and Table I), `RAP-FedWMMSE` executes 3 warm-up epochs of 4 blocks per drop ($12 \text{ warm-up blocks} \times 6 \text{ layers} = 72$ unfolded layer evaluations plus a 61-point $\tau$ grid search) before evaluating the single test coherence block ($6$ layer evaluations).
